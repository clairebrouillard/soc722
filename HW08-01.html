In an additive model, the prediction lines for different values of the other variable are always parallel. Explain in 2–3 sentences what the parallelism means substantively. What assumption is the additive model making about how hs_grad and inc1000 jointly affect life expectancy?

The prediction lines in the additive model are parallel because the lines all have the same slopes since there is no interaction term. This means that the slopes remain constant no matter the high school graduation rate and income; the three lines only differ in their intercepts. This model assumes that the effects of high school graduation rate and income on life expectancy do not change based on each other.

The interaction coefficient is negative, which means that the effect of income increases as the effect of high school graduation decreases and vice versa. The companion explains that the AIC penalty of \(2k\) corresponds to the expected increase in out-of-sample deviance per extra parameter. In your own words, what problem is the penalty term meant to solve? Why can’t we just pick the model with the lowest deviance (i.e., the best in-sample fit)?

The penalty term is meant to account for how much worse the additional parameters will make the deviance. Using the model with the lowers deviance may result in the model being overfitted, or overly complicated.
